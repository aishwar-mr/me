How the screener works

MARS is the assistant in the corner of every page. It asks about your role, then says whether Aishwar is a fit, including when the answer is no.

It does not decide anything. The verdict is computed by your own browser from the rules below, which were written before anyone used the tool and cannot be changed after the fact. The rubric is rubric.json, the scoring is screener.js, and the tests are screener.test.js. Check the answer against them.

The gates

A gate is a single fact about a role that ends the screen. FAIL stops scoring entirely, because no amount of fit elsewhere is allowed to outweigh it. NEAR means it misses on paper but the conversation is still worth having.

The weighted criteria

These run only when every gate clears. Each scores 0 to 10 and is multiplied by its weight. Strong is 70 percent or above, partial is 45 to 69.

CriterionWeightScale
C1 Problem ambiguity30 = fully specced before handover, 10 = nobody yet knows what the question is

C3 Team size20 = 200+ people, 10 = under 10 people

C5 Quantitative content20 = no numbers involved, 10 = the core of the job is quantitative

C8 Autonomy expected20 = work is assigned and reviewed daily, 10 = expected to find the work and decide what matters

Where these rules are probably wrong

Published for the same reason the WorldQuant page publishes a failed control test. A screening system that only reports its successes is not worth trusting.

  • G6 low risk

    Some founders will hire a strong part-timer and convert them on graduation. A NEAR here is often worth a conversation, and a FAIL is occasionally negotiable if the start date is soft.

  • G1 high risk

    Early-stage founders routinely write '3+ years' in a posting and hire someone with none of it because they liked how the person thought. A NEAR here is very often still worth a conversation. Recalibrated 2026-09-05 from PASS 0-2 / FAIL 5+ to PASS 0-1 / FAIL 4+, because zero full-time years should not read as a clean pass against a two-year requirement.

  • G3 medium risk

    'Who gets paged' separates pre-scale from post-scale companies well, but a founder who has never had an incident may answer as though they are post-scale when they are not.

  • G4 medium risk

    Job postings describe scope aspirationally. A role written as narrow is sometimes broad in practice at a small company, and this gate takes the posting at its word.
