1) Digital Product Passport — Sustainability & traceability | 2026 | Research & design | Work in progress | /work/digital-product-passport
A take-back passport for soft furnishings under the EU Digital Product Passport rules — work in progress.
Scope: A voluntary take-back passport for soft furnishings (sofas, curtains, rugs), written as a VJTI project report. Sits alongside, not inside, the first EU apparel act (expected to cover finished apparel with at least 80% textile fibre). EU registry live since 20 July 2026; product data stays with each company, reached through a QR code. Nothing binding until the delegated act, expected Q4 2027, mandatory use around 2029. Hardest data sits with Tier 2–3 suppliers (spinning, weaving, dyeing).
Problem: The EU has fixed how DPPs work but not yet what a textile passport must contain (~3 years to prepare). Tracking only tells you where products go — it doesn't make anyone return them; without a recycler's write-back a passport proves an item was scanned, not recycled.
Approach: Checked DPP research (systematic review of 87 papers + a 2026 review) against the live regulatory picture. Unique IDs, standardised data, traceability, batch-level passports held up; centralised data and item-level-by-default did not; blockchain as key enabler was weakened (law requires only open standards + QR code).
Solution: Built around handover scans rather than continuous tracking: dual-frequency tag (UHF RFID + NFC) sewn into the deck; timestamped scan at each handover (factory, delivery, repair, resale, pickup, recycler intake); a reason to return (buy-back credit, trade-in discount or free pickup); recycler write-back against the same tag ID (condition, weight by stream, route); one data card per model listing components, materials, joins, recovery blockers.
Result: Pending — nothing tested yet. Three steps: sign off sofa/curtain/rug data card with a recycler; 50-unit tag trial targeting >95% read rate; pilot a few hundred units measuring return rate and recovered weight per unit.

2) Track Monitoring — Rail infrastructure | 2025 | Team member — architecture & hybrid localisation | /work/track-monitoring
A low-cost, contactless track-monitoring prototype for Indian Railways. Smart India Hackathon '25 — 1st out of 80 teams (inter-college).
Problem: Current ITMS tech deployed by Indian Railways relies on closed-source Russian systems costing upwards of ₹180 crore — high procurement/maintenance costs, limited customisation, vendor lock-in, inefficient defect localisation (poor GPS accuracy).
My contribution: helped develop a scalable, compact, plug-and-play architecture analysing track geometry parameters; assisted in implementing a hybrid localisation system fusing GPS and IMU sensors for uninterrupted chainage mapping.
Approach: compact contactless rig on ITMS principles — laser, ultrasonic and IMU sensors plus ESP32 camera; Python, OpenCV and a neural network on a Raspberry Pi; edge computing flags faults in real time, cloud handles long-term trends. Evaluated EN 13848 standards and RDSO accuracy requirements. Prototyped and tested live on Central Railways tracks in Mumbai.
Solution: modular open software stack (geometry, rail-profile, acceleration subsystems deployable in full or part). Computer vision flags broken fasteners from camera frames; IMU and ultrasonic readings flag misalignment — either triggers a dashboard alert and an SMS.
Results: SIH '25 1st of 80 teams (inter-college); field-tested on live Central Railway track in Mumbai end to end. Projected cost ₹180 crore (imported) → ₹30 crore (indigenous). Complied with EN 13848 Part 1 & 2 and RDSO TM/IM/448 (Rev 1: 2023) during testing. Advances Atmanirbhar Bharat (data sovereignty, no foreign vendor lock-in).
Challenges: low accuracy of standard GPS for precise defect localisation. Next: team proposed integrating RFID tags directly on tracks to improve chainage-mapping accuracy.
